Zettel · the guide

Your most important conversation is already written. This is the instrument that plays it. Three verbs: communicate, ruminate, execute.

move — travel the timeline

The layer t
Everything summons from below and recedes with esc. The ground stays a calm thread.
The waveform
The whole conversation as a track — your voice above the line, theirs below. Click or drag anywhere to jump. Mouse-wheel zooms time around the cursor; “all” restores.
Play space
The conversation plays itself, skipping silences longer than a day. The needle crosses the track; the played years turn amber.
Go to a date g · ends of history homeend
A month-and-year grid, one click to land.
Move by message jk · by pin np
Arrows work too. Selection is where the quiet buttons appear.

mark — leave traces

The dialect: 📌 decisions · 🔥 ideas · ❓ questions · 🩷 keepsakes · ✅ done · 👍 agreed
Send them in iMessage like you already do — inline or as a tapback — and they become glowing jump points on the waveform, each a distinct shape (diamond, circle, triangle, heart, tick, square) so they read even in pure grayscale. The whole vocabulary iMessage left on the table, made navigable.
Pin back into the thread 📌 on a selected message
Two taps sends a real “📌 ‘quote’ 〰️” message into the actual conversation through Messages itself — both of you see it in Messages, and Zettel renders it as a breathing ember card. The archive file itself is never written.
Reignite ↻ on a selected message
Bring a moment back to attention. Two taps sends a gentle “still on this” nudge into the thread — a reminder for them — and re-lights the moment for you, glowing until it's handled.
Your next message the dashed bubble at the stream's foot
Compose where the message will land. The bubble is dashed while it's only a proposal — two taps send a real iMessage, no emoji, no signature — and the landed message glows amber as it joins the record, so you see exactly where your words went.
Magic ink ✎ hand · in the bubble
Write with the pen; read it back and your ink becomes words — read entirely on your own Mac, never sent anywhere. The words appear for your eyes first, editable, and only then can the two taps send them.
Margins a ✎
A private note on any message — your ink, never leaves this Mac. Lines that start with [] become living checkboxes.
Notes on the track double-click the waveform
Pin a thought to a moment in time itself, no message required. Hover the open circle to read it.
Chapters
Name the eras. They sit on the waveform like sections of a track; click a title to travel.

extract — take things out

Search that works
"exact phrase" from:me from:them before:2024-01-01 after:2023-06-01 has:link has:image has:voice has:mark in:all — that last one searches every conversation you've ever had. Even words that were only spoken: voice memos transcribe on this Mac, then search like text.
Voice memos transcribe
Play them in place; one click transcribes locally (whisper — nothing leaves the machine). Transcripts show, search, and export.
Timestamp links y ⌗
A link to an exact moment, to the second. On the other person's Mac it opens the same moment in their own archive.
Share cards x
A still, beautiful artifact of one moment with a little context — the only thing that ever leaves is what you choose to send.
Export e · copy one message c
High-fidelity transcripts of any range — copy, download, or hand straight to Claude with a framing note. Facets index every link, image, reply-thread, and mark.
Wrapped
The stat sheet: streaks, busiest days, who opens the mornings — over any window, by year or by month. keep as image saves it as a card you can send — numbers only, never words or names.

inhabit — where it lives

Evenings amber
The calm register: warm paper, bigger serif, no chrome. Run with --share and the terminal prints one key-guarded link for a tablet on your wifi — unencrypted, so trusted home wifi only.
Back up your ink
On your Mac, every note, mark, chapter, and pen stroke you've made downloads as a zip — the archive itself never needs backing up (it is read-only, always), but this is yours. Not in this demo: marks you make here live in this browser only.

By default, nothing leaves your Mac — no account, no server of ours. The archive is read-only, forever; your margins stay private on your machine; the only words that leave are the ones you send with two deliberate taps. Turn the wifi off: everything still works.

Three exceptions exist, each a door only you can open. Your wifi — --share serves your archive to a device on your own network, plain HTTP, a wifi you trust. Your folder — if you and one other person choose to co-annotate a thread, only the notes you each explicitly promote sync, through a folder in a cloud you choose and control; those bytes pass through that company's servers, not ours; revoke the folder anytime. Your summons — when you call on an outside mind (a question to Claude, a parcel tracker, a forecast), exactly what you selected travels to that one service, and the summons shows you the payload first. Nothing is scanned, watched, or sent without your two taps.

Every exception is a loan, not an amendment: as capability comes home to your Mac, the crossings retire.
